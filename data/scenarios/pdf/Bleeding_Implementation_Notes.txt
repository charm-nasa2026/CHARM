## STORY
Sarah, a fictional astronaut in transit to Mars, experiences external bleeding after an accidental equipment-related maintenance injury. She initially remains stable, but a crew-reported continuation of bleeding coincides with rising resting HR and a gradual decline in blood pressure. Later, serious symptoms and hypotension require a CRITICAL crew emergency response. Ground communication temporarily becomes unavailable, but the app should maintain locally available information and the emergency state without inventing new readings. After mission-protocol-led medical care, the crew reports bleeding control and Sarah's vital signs improve, followed by monitored recovery.

## CRITICAL SCIENTIFIC AND PRODUCT RESTRICTIONS
- Bleeding is NOT automatically detectable from HR, SpO2, BP, or a wearable stream alone. The event comes from a crew-entered bleeding observation. Make the source visibly clear.
- Vital-sign trends can support assessment of circulatory instability, but this scenario is not a validated hemorrhage detector and cannot estimate blood-loss volume.
- Severe symptoms and low BP together with continuing bleeding lead to the critical state; no single HR or SpO2 value automatically diagnoses shock.
- Do NOT treat a normal SpO2 as proof that significant blood loss is absent.
- Hemoglobin and hematocrit columns are intentionally empty in all rows. An early normal result would not reliably exclude acute bleeding; do not synthesize CBC results or percentages of volume loss.
- No image or graphic wound depiction is requested; show non-graphic interface text only.
- All numeric values are SIMULATED DEMO numbers, not NASA participant recordings or real medical patient data.
- Do not invent drugs, dosages, transfusion volumes, invasive procedures, emergency kit contents, or automatic treatment capability.
- Emergency management is led by trained crew under authorized mission medical protocol, not by the app.
- Keep existing scenarios (arrhythmia, dehydration, respiratory infection, SANS, bone health, etc.) intact.
